Close Cords

Privacy Policy

Effective 11 September 2026 Applies to the Close Cords app for iPhone and Android

The short version

  • Close Cords has no servers and no accounts. Everything about you lives on your phone.
  • Its whole purpose is to share the song you're playing with people physically near you, over Bluetooth. That only happens after you've turned broadcasting on, and you can turn it off any time.
  • We never use your location. Bluetooth range is the only "where" the app knows.
  • Songs you see from other people are kept for one hour, then deleted automatically.
  • We don't sell data, show ads, or send you email.

1Who we are

Close Cords is made by Paul Yazbeck. If anything in this policy is unclear, or you want to ask about your data, contact closecords.app@gmail.com.

2How Close Cords handles data

Close Cords works differently from most apps, and that shapes everything below.

  • No servers. We don't operate a backend. Nothing you do in the app is uploaded to us, and we have no database of users. The only network requests the app makes are to Spotify, Apple Music, and the image servers that host album artwork.
  • No accounts. You don't sign up, and we don't ask for your email, phone number, or password. Your identity in the app is a display name and a randomly generated alias that exist only on your phone.
  • Device to device. The information you choose to share travels directly from your phone to nearby phones over Bluetooth Low Energy. It doesn't pass through us.

Because of this, we are not in a position to see, store, or access your data. What follows describes what the app itself does on your device.

4Sharing what you're playing with people nearby

This is the feature Close Cords exists for, so here is exactly what it does.

What is shared

When broadcasting is on, your phone advertises a small packet over Bluetooth Low Energy that other Close Cords users within range can read. It contains:

  • your alias, and your display name if Anonymous Mode is off;
  • the title, artist, and album artwork link of the track you're playing right now;
  • which service it's playing on (Spotify or Apple Music).

That's the complete list. It does not include your location, your account details, your listening history, or anything else.

Who can see it

Only people running Close Cords within Bluetooth range of your phone — typically around 10 to 15 metres (30 to 50 feet), and less through walls — and only while you are broadcasting. If broadcasting is off, nobody can see you, in any range. When it's on, anyone with the app in that range can see what you've chosen to share; it isn't limited to friends or contacts. Bluetooth signals can in principle be read by other Bluetooth devices nearby, so treat what you broadcast as visible to whoever is physically close to you.

When it happens

Only after you've turned broadcasting on. The first time, the app shows you a screen describing what's shared and asks you to confirm. Until you tap Start broadcasting on that screen, nothing is sent — choosing Not now keeps broadcasting off, and any later attempt to turn it on brings the same screen back. Broadcasting also stops when the app isn't running.

How to stop

Tap the radar button in the app, or turn off Broadcasting Signal in the Profile tab. It stops immediately. You can also switch on Anonymous Mode to keep broadcasting under your alias only, with your display name withheld.

5Spotify

If you connect Spotify, you sign in through Spotify's own login page; Close Cords never sees your Spotify password. Spotify then gives the app limited permissions, each of which is used for one thing:

  • user-read-currently-playing and user-read-playback-state — to know what you're playing, so it can be shown and broadcast, and to know which playback controls Spotify currently allows.
  • user-modify-playback-state — so the play, pause, skip and shuffle buttons in the app control your Spotify.
  • user-read-recently-played — to show your last track when nothing is currently playing.
  • user-read-private — to read your display name and whether you have Spotify Premium, which playback control requires.

The sign-in credentials Spotify issues are stored in your phone's secure keychain and used only to talk to Spotify on your behalf. We don't share anything from your Spotify account with anyone. The track you're playing is shared with nearby users only as described in section 4.

Disconnecting. Tap Spotify Connected in the Profile tab to disconnect. This deletes the stored credentials and the Spotify data the app was holding, and the app stops reading from your Spotify account. You can also revoke Close Cords from your Spotify account settings at any time.

Spotify's handling of your account is governed by Spotify's Privacy Policy. Album artwork and track details shown in the app are provided by Spotify, and links open in Spotify.

6Apple Music

If you connect Apple Music, iOS asks you to allow Close Cords access to Media & Apple Music. With that permission the app can see what the Music app is playing, look up album artwork in the Apple Music catalogue, and control playback. It does not read your library or listening history beyond the current track.

Your Apple Music sign-in is handled by iOS and Apple; the app receives a token it uses only to talk to Apple Music on your behalf. You can revoke access at any time in Settings › Privacy & Security › Media & Apple Music. Apple's handling of your account is governed by Apple's Privacy Policy.

7Bluetooth, and why there's no location

Close Cords uses Bluetooth Low Energy for two things: to broadcast the packet described in section 4, and to listen for the same packet from others. Discovery works by proximity alone. The app never requests, reads, or infers your geographic location — there is no GPS, no map, and no record of where you've been.

On Android 12 and later, the app declares its Bluetooth scanning as not used for location, so the system doesn't ask for location permission. On older Android versions the operating system requires location permission for any Bluetooth scan; if you're prompted there, that is an Android requirement — the app still never uses your location.

The app may keep scanning and broadcasting briefly while in the background so that you can be discovered with your phone in your pocket. Broadcasting stops when the app is closed.

8Historical Cords

When you're near someone who is broadcasting, their alias (or display name) and the track they were playing appear in the Close Cords feed and are kept in Historical Cords so you can look back at who you crossed paths with. Each entry is deleted automatically one hour after you were last near that person. You can also clear the whole list at any time with the Clear button. This list never leaves your phone.

9Crash reporting

To find and fix bugs, the app sends a report to Sentry when it crashes. A report contains technical details — the error, the screen you were on, your device model and operating system version, and the app version. It does not include your name, alias, what you were playing, who you were near, or anything from your Spotify or Apple Music accounts; the app removes that information before anything is sent. Crash reports are not linked to an identity, and we don't use them for anything other than fixing the app.

10What we don't collect

  • Your location, at any precision.
  • Your contacts, photos, calendar, or files.
  • Your email address or phone number.
  • Advertising identifiers. There are no ads and no ad tracking.
  • Analytics about how you use the app.

We don't sell or rent any data, and we have none to sell.

11Your choices and deleting your data

Because everything lives on your phone, you're in direct control of it:

  • Stop sharing: turn off broadcasting, or turn on Anonymous Mode.
  • Change your name or alias: Profile tab. Tap New to generate a fresh alias.
  • Disconnect Spotify or Apple Music: Profile tab. Disconnecting Spotify deletes the stored sign-in immediately.
  • Clear Historical Cords: the Clear button, or wait an hour.
  • Delete everything: delete the app. This removes your profile, settings, and history. We recommend disconnecting Spotify first so its stored sign-in is cleared right away; otherwise it's cleared the next time the app is installed.

If you're in a region with data-protection rights (such as the EU, UK, or California), those rights apply. In practice, because we hold no data about you on our systems, there is nothing for us to access, export, or erase on your behalf — the tools above give you that control directly. If you have a question we can help with, contact closecords.app@gmail.com.

12Children

Close Cords is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age for a Spotify or Apple Music account in your country, if higher), and we don't knowingly collect information from them. The app shares what you're listening to with strangers nearby, which isn't appropriate for young children.

13Security

Spotify credentials are stored in the platform's secure keychain (iOS Keychain or Android Keystore). All communication with Spotify and Apple Music uses HTTPS. Bluetooth broadcasts are, by design, readable by nearby devices — that is how the app works — so the packet is limited to the items in section 4 and never includes credentials or identifiers beyond your chosen name.

14Changes to this policy

If we change what the app does with data, we'll update this page and the effective date at the top. For a material change — anything that shares more than described here — the app will tell you and ask again before it takes effect.
